The second call does start fresh with a new argument. But the first call is not replaced. It is paused, waiting, with all of its local variables intact.

Each paused call sits on a stack. The most recent one finishes first, so the after lines unwind in reverse.

He sits on the edge of the composer, out of the reading column.enter to send
  1. Dragging him is a way to ask. Drop him on the thing you do not follow and he points at it and offers three questions about it. Picking one quotes that passage into the composer for you to review and send. It is the existing quote feature with a handle on it.
  2. He never answers for Keating. On a question he gives a hint, not the answer. Everything he offers is also in the menu and the selection toolbar, so nothing depends on finding him.
  3. He reacts to the lesson, not to the clock. Sending, writing, a right answer, a wrong one, a new material: each has its own animation, and right answers rotate between four so it does not repeat. When he carries flashcards to Materials you learn where they went.
  4. He stays off the text. His seat is the composer's edge in the gutter beside the reading column. Put him on a code block and he goes home when you send. On narrow screens he is the small avatar until you tap him.
  5. You can turn him down. "Give me room" leaves only a peek. Settings gets Companion: Lively, Calm or Off. Reduced motion keeps his face and drops the animation; try the switch above.
  6. Cursor things. His eyes follow the pointer and widen when it is close. They follow the caret while you type. Shake him while dragging and he lands dizzy. Throw him and he bounces off the walls.
