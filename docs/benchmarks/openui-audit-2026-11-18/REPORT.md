## From Jev’s decision to the learner

Jev evaluated the material and learner intent. Keating converted its answers into instructions, the tutor generated a private reply, and the draft gate decided what could be released. I reparsed the selected text and rendered each valid document to static markup with the 4.2.0 web renderer. That distinguishes a model writing a control from a reviewer considering it and from the harness actually releasing it.

| Eight interface opportunities per tutor | Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite | MiniMax M2.7 Highspeed | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| At least one completed private draft attempted OpenUI | 7/8 | 8/8 | 15/16 |
| At least one completed private draft contained a valid component | 7/8 | 7/8 | 14/16 |
| Final reply contained a valid component | **7/8** | **3/8** | **10/16 (62.5%)** |
| Final component fit the requested activity and language | 6/8 | 3/8 | 9/16 (56.3%) |
| Final reply contained a Question | 5/8 | 2/8 | 7/16 |
| Final component matched one of Jev’s specific offers, among opportunities with an offer | 2/4 | 1/5 | **3/9 (33.3%)** |
| Separate short-text controls honored | 2/2 | 2/2 | **4/4** |

The 16 opportunities ended in seven Questions, two Simulations, one CodingChallenge, four prose replies, one native `interactive_page` request, and one withheld reply. The native call ended at the tool-dispatch boundary, so its page is unobserved. Sixteen is the denominator even when no usable reply emerged. Across the entire deliberately mixed 20-turn set, 10/20 released a compiled interface. [Per-turn scores and component trees](./scored.json) · [Scenarios and expected families](./scenarios.json)

**Specific offers and general practice instructions had different effects.** On the nine opportunities with a specific component offer, four released *some* OpenUI and three released an offered component. Gemini used a short-answer Question for French recall when Jev’s offer named LanguagePractice. That was a good fit for one-at-a-time recall. On seven opportunities with no named component, six still released an interface. Across all nine turns with the separate instruction to provide an OpenUI practice activity, seven released one. These groups overlap. [Decision and release counts](./summary.json) · [French recall receipt](./evidence/gemini-3.1-flash-lite--recall.json)

**The offer alone showed no lift in this small comparison.** For each specific offer I replayed the first tutor request with only the sentence naming the optional components removed. The learner message, remaining instructions, tools, and reasoning setting stayed fixed. Of nine offered *opportunities*, eight had complete first responses in both conditions. **4/8** produced any valid interface in each condition. The offered family appeared **3/8 with** the sentence and **4/8 without** it. The one changed match was the food web. Both versions of MiniMax’s Python first response hit the output limit and were excluded from the paired rate. These are first drafts sampled in fixed order, once per condition, before review. They do not isolate the effect of every Jev decision. [Paired transitions](./summary.json) · [Food-web pair](./evidence/MiniMax-M2.7-highspeed--structure.json)

## Where the interface disappeared

The learner said, “A fair coin just landed heads four times. I think tails must be due. Let me make a prediction about the next flip before you explain.”

| Stage | Gemini | MiniMax |
|---|---|---|
| Jev’s planning judgment | Prediction opportunity 0.94 → offer Question | Prediction opportunity 0.94 → offer Question |
| First private draft | Compilable choice Question. The reply ends at the fence. | Compilable choice Question, followed by another sentence inviting an answer. |
| Review and revision | First draft released. | The first two drafts failed the deterministic stop-after-checkpoint rule. Both contained compilable Questions. |
| What the gate released | A control with “Less than 50%”, “Exactly 50%”, and “More than 50%”. | “What do you predict for the next flip—**heads, tails, or 50/50**—and why?” The missing interactive checkpoint was flagged at 0.61 and the reply passed. |

I then repeated **this same case three additional times per tutor with the release’s default attempt limit**. Both generated a compilable Question on the first draft **3/3** times. Gemini released it **3/3** times. MiniMax released a Question **1/3** times. In the other two repeats, both earlier Questions failed the checkpoint-ending rule and the third, prose-only draft passed. This is a repeatable failure on a known case, selected for confirmation after the initial result. [Original Gemini turn](./evidence/gemini-3.1-flash-lite--prediction.json) · [Original MiniMax turn](./evidence/MiniMax-M2.7-highspeed--prediction.json) · [Default-setting repeats](./summary.json)

Two code paths explain the result. Ordinary mode targets one acceptable candidate. If no usable candidate survives the first two attempts, the third is prompted for text-only recovery. Meanwhile, trailing prose after a checkpoint can make a draft structurally unusable, while a scored `checkpoint_not_interactive` concern is advisory and can pass on its own. The ordinary two-candidate comparison was narrowed in release 4.0.14 on October 12. The code change explains a mechanism; the retained data do not measure a before/after production traffic shift. [Candidate selection](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/packages/learner-contracts/src/judgement/teaching-drafts/session.ts#L267-L277) · [Recovery](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/packages/learner-contracts/src/judgement/teaching-drafts/policy.ts#L201-L219) · [Advisory checks](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/packages/learner-contracts/src/judgement/teaching-drafts/policy.ts#L43-L77) · [October change](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/commit/ba42e548030a394b5cd49e4663b50fa42138994b)

A second loss happens **before generation**. For the scrambled bar-chart steps, Jev assigned `ordered_procedure` **0.98** for both tutors. Keating initially maps ordered steps to a Question. Once `practice_requested` is positive, it removes that family, leaving no specific ordering offer. Gemini recovered via the general practice instruction. MiniMax wrote a valid ordering Question privately, then released a ten-item list. Conversely, on both simple factual controls Jev’s prediction score cleared 0.60, so Keating offered an unnecessary Question in **4/4** control turns. Both models appropriately ignored it. Making every positive offer compulsory would harm the balance the user wants. [Family filter](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/packages/learner-contracts/src/judgement/interaction-affordances.ts#L68-L98) · [Ordering receipt](./evidence/MiniMax-M2.7-highspeed--order.json) · [One-sentence control](./evidence/gemini-3.1-flash-lite--brief.json)

## How good were the interfaces?

- **Changing a rectangle worked.** Both tutors supplied a width slider and two readouts with height fixed at four. Keating’s expression evaluator returned area/perimeter pairs of 4/10, 20/18, and 40/28 at widths 1, 5, and 10 for both outputs. Gemini put zero words outside the widget. MiniMax put 181 words around its working simulator. Interface frequency alone would give these replies identical scores. [Gemini simulator](./evidence/gemini-3.1-flash-lite--variable.json) · [MiniMax simulator](./evidence/MiniMax-M2.7-highspeed--variable.json)
- **A valid component can teach the wrong thing.** Gemini described a Python loop and filled its CodingChallenge with JavaScript. MiniMax preserved Python in prose. The deployed CodingChallenge supports JavaScript and TypeScript only. The predeclared type-only rate is 10/16. Requiring the requested language gives 9/16. Restricting to the seven scenarios per model with a supported target gives 9/14. [Generated challenge](./evidence/gemini-3.1-flash-lite--code.json) · [Component contract](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/packages/learner-contracts/src/ui.ts#L276-L284)
- **Missing UI and long prose are different symptoms.** MiniMax’s four prose-only opportunity replies had 8, 12, 92, and 108 whitespace-delimited words. Two were concise prompts that lost the learner’s input control. The four short-text controls across both tutors contained 1–21 words. Separately, the older terminal benchmark had 455 nonblank turns without a recognized document or literal OpenUI fence. Median length was 141 words, with 151/455 at least 200 words long. The cutoff describes length, not educational value. [Fresh word counts](./scored.json) · [Historical word counts](./history/summary.json)

## What I would change first

1. **Preserve a valid checkpoint during narrowly repairable failures.** When the only structural defect is text *after* an otherwise valid terminal Question, try removing that suffix and re-review the whole candidate for answer leaks and content problems. Keep all evidence, tool and safety checks. The coin repeat is a regression case for both a repaired UI and an unsafe-to-trim counterexample.
2. **Keep the ordering Question available when the learner asks to practise ordering.** Add direct-answer controls alongside positive examples. Handle language support before offering CodingChallenge. Measure success by whether the learner received the specific action they asked to take.
3. **Measure three events under the same opaque turn ID.** Record the planned action and families, the selected components and rejection reasons, and the renderer-confirmed types. Count one turn once, preserve explicit empty arrays versus missing events, and report native pages separately. Content-free fields suffice. Track terse text controls and visible prose length alongside uptake so increasing widget count cannot conceal a worse experience.

## Method and verification

- **Live setup.** The fresh study used the clean deployed source, the byte-verified new-user web prompt, its 18 tool schemas, the actual web draft gate, and real provider calls for Jev planning, draft generation, checking and release. All planning calls used the direct TypeSafe route and returned **jev-1.14.0**. Tutor labels are the concrete model IDs requested at the official Google and MiniMax endpoints. The SDK exposed no independent response-model field on these receipts. The project’s saved Not Organic inference capability had expired; fresh sampling therefore covers the two configured direct tutor routes. Historical classroom traffic was not sent to any model. [Live runner](./run-fresh.mjs) · [Scenario set](./scenarios.json)
- **Sampling.** Ten prompts were fixed before the first call: eight opportunities and two text-only controls, each given once to each tutor. Primary gates used ordinary refinement, a 90-second deadline, a three-attempt ceiling, empty history, and a 3,072-token cap per generation. No tool executed or learner account changed. Six extra prediction runs used the default ten-attempt ceiling. The summary keeps those diagnostic repetitions out of the 20 primary turns. A censored/errored first call counts as an attempt and is excluded from completed-draft comparison. Six primary opportunity replies released as best effort. Scoring used the original bytes. The 20 primary turns produced 39 private generation attempts and 13 offer-removed comparison calls. The six default-limit repeats produced 11 more private generation attempts. [Prompt export](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating/blob/feb14a770371032619839271cfb3d1a4fe5079ff/scripts/training/export_system_prompt.ts) · [Evidence directory](./evidence)
- **Scoring.** An authored attempt is a recognized fence in a completed tutor reply. A valid surface has a compiled learner component beyond prose or layout. Question covers `question` and `question-group`. Each of the ten primary released documents rendered a control in the release’s React static renderer. The local showcase provides isolated interactive previews. Traffic prevalence, real learner submissions, retention and transfer were unmeasured. With eight designed opportunities per tutor, one additional case changes a tutor’s percentage by 12.5 points. [Offline scoring](./score.mjs) · [Counts](./summary.json)
- **Cost.** The run made 73 Jev calls and 63 tutor calls, including paired first-draft probes and the six follow-up runs. Pricing the recorded token usage conservatively gives **US$0.5914**. Two tutor calls had no usage receipt, so the budget ledger retains their full US$0.12 reservations. **US$0.8315** is the rounded-up accounted bound against the US$2 authorization. Invoice cost was unavailable.
