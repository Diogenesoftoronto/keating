# Does the learner get the interface the tutor wrote?

**November 18, 2026.** We found two cases where the tutor wrote a usable prediction field and Keating showed the learner a prose question. Both decision models also helped tutors write better replies on some of the same learner prompts. The evidence supports keeping decision guidance and changing how Keating chooses among drafts. [Try the original replies](index.html) · [View the classroom demonstration](classroom/index.html) · [Read Fable’s guide](field-guide.html) · [Download the results](final.json)

## How often it happened

Four learners asked to predict paper layers, vary irrigation flow, repair a Python loop or get copper’s chemical symbol. Each prompt went to MiniMax M2.7 Highspeed and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite under each of three conditions. None of the four prompts asked for an interface by name. The irrigation learner explicitly declined a quiz. The guided conditions used the ordinary v2.3.3 web planning and release path. The unguided condition sent the ordinary tutor prompt and tools and returned its first response. Four requests to the Gemini tutor returned HTTP 503. All eight unguided calls returned. Two of eight scheduled results were unknown in each guided condition. Among the 18 action assignments, **13 of 15 observed first drafts contained the intended control, and 12 of 15 did at publication (80%)**. Three had no observed reply. Across all four tasks, fenced OpenUI appeared in **8/20 first drafts** and **7/20 public replies (35%)**. The five editable Python replies also gave learners a control. The 12 replies with controls had a **median of 55 surrounding words**, ranging from zero to 240. Across six fully observed pairs for each decision model, Jev improved first-draft task fit on three, worsened it on one and tied on two. Gemini Decision improved one and tied on five. Two pairs in each comparison had missing guided results. Guided generation also chooses reasoning effort. ## Where the controls went

Every guided primary tutor request carried its assigned decision directives. This included **10/10** completed Jev-guided drafts, **7/7** completed Gemini Decision-guided drafts and the four calls that returned a provider error. Ten turns received a named component offer. Among seven with a completed draft, five used an offered family on their first draft and four did so publicly. For Jev this changed from **3/3 to 2/3**. For Gemini Decision it stayed **2/4**. The three missing opportunities remain visible in the exported denominator. An offer of multiple families counts as followed if the tutor uses any offered family. The learner’s actual goal has its own score. [Decision and draft rows](analysis.json)

The two losses both came from “Before you tell me, I’d like to guess.” Under Jev, MiniMax’s first draft contained a working QuestionGroup. The reviewer flagged repetition around the field and the final fallback was a plain question. Under Gemini Decision, Flash produced both approved prose and an approved QuestionGroup. The final chooser picked the prose. Two of six guided primary turns that compiled an OpenUI control in any draft lost it before selection. [See the Jev turn](index.html#paper-fold--minimax--jev) · [See the Gemini Decision turn](index.html#paper-fold--flash--google)

Fable traced both selection paths and changed them to retain an eligible prediction field when the plan calls for a learner answer. Replaying the stored replies and judgments recovered both Questions. The Jev case retained its best-effort notice and the Gemini Decision case used its already approved draft. Fifteen other recorded outcomes stayed the same, and 21 opt-out, leakage, uncertainty, syntax and irrelevant-task counterexamples kept the disqualified Question out. Use **After the fix · offline replay** on either case in the showcase. [Changes and counterexamples](prediction-replay/README.md) · [Exact replay outputs](prediction-replay/patched.json)

Fit depended on what the learner could do **and** what they had declined. All four observed irrigation replies provided a slider for six-minute volume. Both guided replies respected “No quiz, thanks.” Both unguided replies still asked the learner to predict. Five public Python replies provided editable starters, and three preserved the learner’s chance to locate the fault independently. The Jev-guided MiniMax and unguided Flash replies supplied unwanted clues. Seven distinct first or final Python starters ran on the supplied list. Six printed an incorrect result and one raised an index error. [Fable’s blind ratings](ratings.json) · [Python execution results](python-execution.json)

## What a learner saw

In one fresh local classroom session a learner asked to vary a stage light’s flash rate over 12 seconds. Jev offered a Simulation. MiniMax wrote two drafts, and the second reached release after 24.123 seconds with an unverified best-effort notice. The browser mounted its slider alongside 47 words of prose. Moving from 6 to 7 flashes per second changed the count from 72 to 84. A second browser opened the saved reply and independently verified that 9 produced 108. Two additional browser sessions replayed exact provider releases through Keating’s classroom. A Python editor accepted an edit and reset to the original source, with 48 words outside the editor. The inspector in Settings, its export, the locally registered WebMCP tool and the CLI agreed. These three purposefully selected sessions establish that the controls can be operated. ### When the learner answers in chat

The v2.3.6 review now checks two separate facts before treating an explanation as feedback. Did the learner answer the preceding question? Does the tutor accurately confirm or correct that answer using the facts in the question? The application retains both sets of probabilities on the private attempt. Strong evidence can make two generic checkpoint findings inapplicable. Independent findings about factual support, host actions, claims of mastery and other unanswered work continue to affect release.

We gave MiniMax the same original tile-bag Question and the same learner answer twice, each time with a single decision model handling planning and review. **Both delivered correct feedback on that learner turn.** Jev released its third private draft, a short confirmation of the 3/4 and 1/4 probabilities. Gemini Decision released the first draft, which gave the same probabilities, added two explanations and proposed an interactive page. The study recorded the proposal. Execution and rendering of that page remain unobserved. These two sessions supplied the recorded chat history and had no open-card record. [Jev-guided reply](study/feedback-v2.3.6/receipts/fresh--confirmation--jev.json) · [Gemini Decision-guided reply](study/feedback-v2.3.6/receipts/fresh--confirmation--google.json)

We also sent each decision model four identical, predeclared review inputs and applied its actual response through Keating’s review logic. Both accepted the original correct feedback. Both rejected a false affirmation and a reply that revealed the answer to a new, unanswered Question. **Both also rejected an accurate correction to a wrong guess.** That gives each model three of four expected review outcomes. Jev had an independent uncertain preference check, and Gemini Decision had insufficient confidence in the correction. The generic answer-disclosure checks consequently remained active. Accurate corrections remain a concrete failure case. [Prespecified cases](study/feedback-v2.3.6/cohort.json) · [Matched review outcomes and probabilities](study/feedback-v2.3.6/review-stage.json)

The four matched pairs share a real Jev planning result to give both reviewers byte-identical input. In the combined session Keating rejected the switch from Jev planning to Gemini review. ### Inspect it in Keating

In **Chat → Settings → Diagnostics → Interface use**, select **Final review · selected tutor replies** and choose **Interface compiled, then dropped** under **Inspect turns**. The two paper-fold rows show which tutor, decision model, offered families and review findings led to each outcome. Open **Final review · original drafts with two decision models** to compare the same tutor and case against its unguided control. You can also import a content-free recording or use **This browser** after a new teaching turn. [Open the two recorded losses](../diagnostics.html?source=review&cue=implicit-action&view=draft-loss)

**Released drafts** shows the winning attempt and its review status. New receipts also say when a requested Question displaced approved prose or was retained under the presentation-only best-effort rule. An older receipt can establish that draft 3 reached the learner and carried a best-effort notice while leaving the selection rule unknown. [How selection is recorded](classroom/README.md)

For a model or script, the read-only `keating.openui_diagnostics` WebMCP tool accepts `{"view":"draft-loss","cue":"implicit-action","limit":5}`. The CLI accepts the equivalent filters. Both report component types, the number of physical learner turns before a match, and which opportunities remain unresolved or unknown. The live stage-light browser returned the same aggregate JSON through both entry points. [Actual WebMCP and CLI result](classroom/live-stage-light/webmcp-and-cli.json) · [Query and schema guide](../../../openui-diagnostics.md)

```sh
rtk bun src/cli/main.ts openui-diagnostics web/src/keating/openui/examples/final-review.json --cue implicit-action --view draft-loss --limit 5
rtk bun src/cli/main.ts openui-diagnostics web/src/keating/openui/examples/final-review-first.json --decision-model gemini-decision-1
```
